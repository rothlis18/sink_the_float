**Critique of Institutional State Pension Funds' Mandates**

1. **Actuarial Discount Rate Constraint Loop:**

Let's denote:
- \( A \) as the fund's assets,
- \( R \) as the actuarial discount rate (6.5%–7.5%),
- \( F \) as the monthly float,
- \( t \) as time in years,
- \( i \) as the market return rate.

The constraint loop can be modeled as follows:

\[ A_{t+1} = A_t + F + A_t \cdot i_t \]
\[ A_t \cdot (1 + R) \geq A_{t+1} \cdot (1 + i_{t+1}) \]

This implies that the fund must achieve a return of at least \( R \) to avoid insolvency. If the market return \( i \) is consistently lower than \( R \), the fund is forced to take on more risk to meet its obligations.

2. **Regulatory and Liquidity Boundaries:**

- **Fiduciary Duty:** Fund managers have a legal obligation to act in the best interests of beneficiaries. This often translates to a risk-averse strategy, but the actuarial discount rate constraint forces them to take on more risk.
- **Liquidity Traps:** Funds must maintain liquidity to meet redemption requests. However, high demand for liquid assets can drive down yields, forcing funds into riskier assets.
- **Fiat Currency Degradation:** Inflation erodes the purchasing power of funds. To maintain the real value of their assets, funds must achieve returns higher than the inflation rate, often leading to riskier allocations.

**Critique of Centralized to Localized Compute Transition**

1. **Algorithmic Enclosure:**

Let's denote:
- \( D \) as the public data pool,
- \( F \) as the semantic filters,
- \( T \) as the telemetry harvesting rate.

The algorithmic enclosure mechanism can be modeled as:

\[ E = D \cdot F \cdot T \]

Where \( E \) represents the extent of enclosure. Centralized monopolies can control public data pools by enforcing ideological compliance guidelines through semantic filters and telemetry harvesting.

2. **Resilience Threshold of Local Edge Networks:**

Let's denote:
- \( N \) as the number of nodes in the local network,
- \( B \) as the network bandwidth,
- \( Q \) as the query rate,
- \( T_b \) as the transaction barrier token price.

The resilience threshold can be modeled as:

\[ R = \frac{N \cdot B}{Q \cdot T_b} \]

For absolute data sovereignty and intellectual autarky, \( R \) must be greater than 1. Local hardware parameters such as processing power, memory, and storage capacity also play a crucial role in establishing a self-sustaining offline data fortress.

3. **Tokenized Transaction Barriers:**

The mathematical boundary of tokenized transaction barriers can be modeled as:

\[ T_b \geq \frac{Cost_{query}}{Value_{data}} \]

Where \( Cost_{query} \) is the cost of querying data and \( Value_{data} \) is the value of the data being queried. The operational perimeter of a self-sustaining offline data fortress is defined by the point where \( T_b \) is high enough to deter external access but low enough to facilitate internal transactions.
