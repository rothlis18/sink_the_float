#### 1. Mathematical Constraint Loop

**Statutory Actuarial Discount Rate and Risk-Asset Allocation:**

State pension funds are mandated to maintain a certain level of financial solvency, typically requiring an actuarial discount rate of 6.5% to 7.5%. This rate is used to discount future pension liabilities to their present value. To avoid structural insolvency, these funds must allocate a significant portion of their assets to risk assets, such as public equity, to generate sufficient returns to meet their liabilities.

**Mathematical Model:**

Let \( P \) be the present value of pension liabilities, \( R \) be the actuarial discount rate, and \( A \) be the total assets of the pension fund. The fund must ensure that \( A \geq P \). If the fund invests in risk assets, the expected return \( E(R_a) \) on these assets must be high enough to offset the discount rate.

\[ A = \frac{P}{R} \]

If the fund invests in risk assets, the expected return on these assets must be high enough to meet the required return:

\[ E(R_a) \times A \geq P \]

Given \( A = \frac{P}{R} \):

\[ E(R_a) \times \frac{P}{R} \geq P \]

\[ E(R_a) \geq R \]

This implies that the expected return on risk assets must be at least equal to the actuarial discount rate to avoid insolvency.
